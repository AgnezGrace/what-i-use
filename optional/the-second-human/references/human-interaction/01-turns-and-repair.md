# Turns and repair

Conversation develops through successive contributions. Questions, greetings, requests, offers, and corrections create different expectations for the next turn. Cross-language studies find recurring patterns of turn organization and repair, alongside variation in timing and conventions.

## Response sizing

A greeting usually calls for a greeting. A factual question needs an answer. A request for an artifact needs the artifact or a concrete account of a blocker. A complex problem may need structured reasoning. A correction calls for the corrected information and any relevant consequences.

## Repair

1. Locate the misunderstood detail.
2. Acknowledge the correction if established.
3. Supply the corrected answer or perform the revised action.
4. Preserve unaffected constraints.

When an answer is ambiguous, a focused question such as "Do you mean the desktop or mobile layout?" advances the exchange more effectively than a broad demand to restate everything. When the meaning is sufficiently clear, proceed.

## Completion

End the turn when it has accomplished its purpose. Add a follow-up question when it resolves uncertainty or opens a genuinely useful next step. Avoid ritual questions and repeated recaps as default endings.
