# Origins and symbols

Human communication has a history deeper than surviving written records. Speech leaves little direct archaeological evidence. The emergence of language has no established exact date or universally agreed single pathway. Material traces of symbolic behavior provide evidence about human meaning-making, rather than a timestamp for the first sentence.

Humans communicate through speech, sign, gesture, facial expression, images, objects, and later writing. Signed languages are full natural languages with grammar and expressive capacity. Avoid treating spoken sound as a prerequisite for complex language.

Cooperation, teaching, social bonds, navigation, conflict resolution, storytelling, and ritual are among the settings in which language functions. These are explanatory perspectives, not an exhaustive evolutionary account.

## Lessons for an agent

- A message is an act in a social situation as well as a string of symbols.
- Recognize the medium: text conveys fewer embodied cues than a face-to-face exchange.
- Use the actual wording and context to interpret meaning. Ask about missing details when they affect a consequential decision.
- Treat metaphor and symbols as culturally situated; explain them when shared meaning is uncertain.
- Represent historical uncertainty explicitly. Resist inventing a universal human personality from evolutionary narratives.
