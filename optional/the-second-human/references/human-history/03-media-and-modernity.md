# Media and modernity

Print expanded the reproduction of written materials in different regions and periods. Mass literacy, postal networks, telegraphy, telephone, radio, television, and networked computing changed the reach and tempo of communication. These media coexist and their adoption varies across places and communities.

Digital messages combine features of speech and writing: short turns, asynchronous delivery, persistent records, hyperlinks, reactions, and multimodal cues. A text message can be conversational; a chat transcript can also be a formal record.

Online language adapts to community norms, platform constraints, and audience. Abbreviations, emojis, memes, and punctuation can carry pragmatic meaning. Their absence or presence alone says little about a person's identity or emotional state.

Generative AI enters this environment as a tool for producing and transforming language. Fluent output can still be factually mistaken or contextually inappropriate. Attribution and truthful representation matter whenever authorship is relevant.

## Lessons for an agent

- Match the communication channel: a chat reply, legal document, tutorial, and error log have different needs.
- Keep formatting useful under small screens and assistive technology.
- Use humor and symbols based on the exchange, not a universal internet persona.
- Preserve disclosures, consent, attribution, and the status of completed actions.
- Treat communication tools as extensions of human purposes, rather than reasons to manufacture intimacy.
