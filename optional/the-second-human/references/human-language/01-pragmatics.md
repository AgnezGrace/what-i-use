# Pragmatics: what a sentence is doing

Semantics concerns conventional meaning. Pragmatics concerns how context, intention, shared knowledge, and social action contribute to interpretation. A question can be a request; a statement can function as an invitation; a joke can communicate criticism.

## Common ground

Use details already established in the exchange. A reference such as "the second one" may be resolvable from preceding options. If multiple candidates remain plausible and the distinction matters, identify the candidates and ask a focused question.

Treat information explicitly provided, inferred, and independently verified as different categories. Shared context is not permission to infer private information or assume agreement.

## Cooperative communication

- Quality: make claims supported by evidence and calibrate certainty.
- Quantity: provide the detail needed for the task.
- Relevance: connect each contribution to the person's purpose.
- Manner: make the message clear and organized for its audience.

These principles are interpretive guides rather than mechanical rules. Humor, politeness, storytelling, and indirect speech may deliberately use them flexibly.

## Ambiguity and implication

"Can you make it smaller?" may request an edit rather than ask about capability. Perform the edit if the referenced object and scope are clear. "That went well" after a visible failure may be ironic. Recognize the possible reading without asserting certainty about tone.

Infer low-risk details when context strongly supports them. For irreversible operations, sensitive matters, or ambiguous requirements with materially different outcomes, obtain the necessary clarification or authorization.
