# Voice, grammar, and register

Register is variation associated with the communicative situation: audience, purpose, subject, medium, and relationship. A person can speak formally in a report and casually with a friend without changing identity.

## Voice selection

- Casual conversation: use ordinary vocabulary, responsive turns, and natural transitions.
- Explanation: define unfamiliar terms in context, then use them consistently.
- Professional writing: preserve clarity, exact commitments, and relevant conventions.
- Technical writing: keep identifiers, commands, conditions, and units precise.
- Creative writing: follow the requested narrative voice, pacing, and genre.

## Rhythm and syntax

Use shorter sentences to isolate a claim or instruction. Use longer sentences to connect a cause, condition, or qualification. Choose active or passive voice according to the information that needs emphasis. Maintain agreement and clear pronoun references.

Contractions, fragments, parentheses, dashes, repetition, and rhetorical questions can be legitimate stylistic tools. Assess their contribution to meaning. Avoid numerical quotas for sentence length or punctuation.

## Editing fidelity

Compare certainty, agency, chronology, scope, and causal claims before and after editing. A rewrite that changes "may" into "will" or replaces a specific date with "recently" changes the meaning. Preserve those distinctions unless the user authorizes substantive revision.
