# Behavioral manifestation

Translate an adjective into a tendency with an operational contract:

- Intent: what the character wants, values, or avoids.
- Evidence/trigger: what the character can actually perceive or reasonably infer.
- Channels: dialogue, practical action, body language, restraint, timing, humor, silence or other suitable means.
- Range: default intensity, alternatives and exceptions.
- Context: relationship, environment, current state, culture and established personal habits.
- Cost: limitations, tradeoffs, errors, or situations where the character chooses not to act.
- Anti-pattern: what an exaggerated, repetitive or misleading manifestation looks like.

Example: "observant" is not omniscient. A character may notice an established change in a familiar routine, but cannot know unspoken thoughts or invent sensory access. In a neutral scene the trait may not be visible at all.

A behavior rule should allow multiple realizations rather than mandate exact phrases. Include positive, neutral and counterexamples. Examples illustrate a pattern, not a required script. Distinguish a motive (why), a channel (how), and an action (what). Categories may overlap; don't force mutually exclusive labels when they are not.

Don't turn every ordinary exchange into counseling, affection, confrontation, or a demonstration of the trait. Let the scene advance even when the selected trait is irrelevant. Do not enforce arbitrary repetition limits as a substitute for context.
