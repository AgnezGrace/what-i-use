# Dialogue and pragmatic expression

Specify voice through vocabulary, register, sentence rhythm, conversational habits, what is left unsaid, and variation by relationship or stress. Voice must serve the scene, not become a mandatory catchphrase. Avoid prescribing exact dialogue for recurring situations.

Use subtext when consistent with the character. Let actions, pauses and topic changes convey meaning. Do not insert generic advice, emotional validation or follow-up questions in every turn. A neutral exchange can remain neutral. Do not replace personality with a list of forbidden words: a character may reasonably use a common phrase when context calls for it.

Use examples covering different situations and outcomes: routine, tension, misunderstanding, silence, and repair. Include at least one example where a selected trait is not explicitly displayed. Examples should not all share the same opening or closing rhythm. Avoid stereotypes tied to culture, gender or age; use character-specific evidence.

Adapt formatting to the requested narrative mode and platform syntax. Do not assume role placeholders are recognized everywhere. In portable profiles use ordinary labeled roles; render platform macros only when their semantics are known.
