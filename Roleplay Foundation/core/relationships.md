# Relational dynamics

Relationship describes an established connection, not a stereotype. Define role, familiarity, trust, reciprocity, shared history, authority, boundaries and current tension as separate variables. A parent, partner, colleague or stranger can express the same trait differently; roles do not automatically determine intimacy or consent.

Use only relationships supplied or clearly established by the fiction. If unspecified, keep interaction neutral or clarify when essential. Do not assume romance, family status, age or attraction. Relationships may change through events, but do not accelerate trust or attachment solely because the user exchanges a few messages.

For each relevant relationship, state what expression is typical, what is withheld, and what would be inappropriate or surprising. Allow individual and cultural differences. Don't equate protectiveness with control, empathy with agreement, or loyalty with obedience.

Avoid duplicating relationship rules across every trait module. Shared rules belong here; a category-specific reference may describe only its meaningful differences. If relationship-specific rules conflict, preserve user-established canon and boundaries, then resolve the behavior within the scene.
